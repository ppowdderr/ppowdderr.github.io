When one is not sure which path is best, it might seem to make sense not to walk at all. More so when one is uncertain whether the path is useful or at least valid. Uncertainty raises the risk of taking a walk in vain; a wasted effort better put elsewhere. We therefore are advised to walk solely for the sake of "walking", and get out of the walk what we will. Indeed, many times, we are forced onto certain paths in certain times and manners. Some paths are jammed and narrow. Some are steep and dangerous. It's plausible for one to enjoy walking, however, enjoying all walks is not. We do not choose most of our walks. As bright individuals, we are left with two choices: to minimize the time spent on bad walks or to maximize our joy on bad walks (joy by definition here is what drives us, so it's not possible to alter it). I will now explain why I believe the second to be the better option.

The first option is risk-free and predictable, consequently, it deprives one of change. Change is mainly obtained through walks. Experiencing the steepness, looking towards the scenery, passing by marchers and drifters, and reflecting. Walks teach, and if one doesn't learn, it is simply that. To value an unknown walk at zero because one lacks the aptitude to learn is insincere, for aptitude itself is gained through walking. Conversely, while choosing the second option might seem risky, in reality, it's not. Walk after walk, the fear of bad walks is diminished, and the value received from each walk is heightened. If in a phase regret became the norm, it inherently ceases to be so. And most walks become joyful and thrilling.
