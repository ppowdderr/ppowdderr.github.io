A ship can cross an ocean on its own. It carries what it needs to keep a direction, and in open water a direction is all it needs. Home, however, is not open water. It is narrow, shallow and rocky, and a ship that has been right for a thousand miles is most likely to be wrong in the last one. It is for this reason that ships are not left to find their way home; they are shown it. One might think being shown is a lesser way of arriving, that a ship needing guidance has failed at being a ship. I believe being shown the way is how the voyage is completed.

A ship's compass is never perfectly true. It is pulled off by the ship's own iron, by the very thing that makes it a ship, and the error cannot be found by looking at the compass, only by comparing it against something outside. Judgement is the same. It is bent by what one carries, and one cannot correct it from within, for the measure is itself bent. Then there is the lighthouse. A lighthouse does not steer the ship, nor does it come to meet it. It stays where it is and signals: "here I am", and from that a ship learns where it is. But a signal is only a signal to one who can read it. A light whose meaning one never learned is just a light, and the learning must happen before the night it is needed. The people and ideas that guide us are much the same: waiting for us all along, yet of no use to those who never learned their language.

Finally, at the mouth of the harbour, a ship takes on a pilot, someone who knows these particular waters. The captain does not stop being the captain, but he admits that knowing the sea is not knowing the harbour. Most of us cross our open seas well enough alone. It is in arriving, in a place, a person, a craft, that we run aground, and it is there that pride in a long voyage tempts us to refuse help. Refusing it is how ships are wrecked close to shore, and the help refused often comes from someone who once crossed the same sea, for pilots are mostly old captains. Ships are shown their way home, yet it is still the ship that has to sail in.
